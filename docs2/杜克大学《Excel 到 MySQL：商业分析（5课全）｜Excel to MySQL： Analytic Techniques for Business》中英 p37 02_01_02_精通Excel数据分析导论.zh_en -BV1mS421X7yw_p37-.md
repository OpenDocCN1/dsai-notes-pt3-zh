The purpose of data analysis is to reduce uncertainty so that human beings can make wiser decisions。

In business， informed decisions are based on analysis of appropriate business metrics。

And seek to increase revenues， maximize profitability， or reduce risk。

When a business decision regarding a particular action must be made。

If given the choice between making the decision under greater uncertainty or less。

 every rational person would choose less uncertainty in business。

 at least we are all uncertainty averse。Of course， in business as in life。

Nearly all important decisions must nevertheless be made under conditions of partial uncertainty。

That is okay， it's the nature of leadership。Although we would all like to be certain。

Accepting partial ignorance。Acknowledging to ourselves how much we don't know。

Is a form of realism that leads to better decision making。In fact。

 realism about uncertainty is essential to avoid excessive risk。

Overestimating uncertainty can lead to delay and paralysis risky in themselves。

While underestimating uncertainty is even more dangerous。

 it is a form of hubris that leads to disaster。During my first 20 years as an entrepreneur and CEO。

 as well as a venture investor and board member with information technology startups offering commercial products and services based on analysis of very big data sets。

I gradually came to believe that most data analysis work is performed without sufficient accountability。

By lack of accountability， I mean that there is no way for outsiders to determine how much reduction in uncertainty is truly offered by the data analytics team's answer。

The team itself probably doesn't know。Because the data analysis process is not transparent。

Everyone is an outsider to it all the way up to the leader who is receiving the data analysis team's report and must make a decision based on it and take responsibility for the consequences。

Is the reduction in uncertainty sufficient to proceed right now to a decision or should we wait and gather more data？

Is this answer capturing the largest possible reduction uncertainty or just a reduction offered by whatever model the data analysis team happened to try first？

And how much uncertainty， exactly。Remains。I came to believe that any well designed data analysis project should measure uncertainty a minimum of twice first。

At the present state of knowledge， before any new data are observed and analyzed。And then again。

 a second time after the technical team has extracted as much information as present data and the best methods can provide。

The difference between these two uncertainty levels。

Will be the reduction in uncertainty or information gain provided by the new data。

 plus the data analysts work。This is the signal for the decision。

It should be presented along with a realistic assessment of the remaining uncertainty。

The residual error or noise that decision makers must live with and choose to act under。

Methods for quantifying uncertainty are well defined in information theory and have come to be applied rigorously to problems of inference through the field known as Bayesian logical data analysis。

Baesianological data analysis methods were largely developed by physicists。

 building on Claude Shannon's fundamental definition of information published in 1948。

The core ideas can be traced back to the pioneering work of E。 T Janes in the 1950s。

 and more recent books on the topic include excellent textbooks by Phil Gregory。

 David McKay and Davederji Sivia， among others。Baesian logical data analysis ideas inform advanced work in machine learning and artificial intelligence。

The most sophisticated companies like Airbnb and Amazon and Google are doing machine learning as a practical implementation of these methods。

Their informational edge is part of the reason for their spectacular success against their less well informed competitors。

However。Information theory ideas are still brand new to most businesses and too often these ideas are first presented to students too late in their education for their fundamental importance to be recognized in university graduate engineering or computer science classes。

Or maybe not at all。I believe that these ideas can and should be taught at the elementary level when introducing the most basic analysis methods such as binary classification and linear regression。

So this course has a strong point of view。It is organized around methods to provide accountability in business applications of data science by offering rigorous。

 consistent ways to quantify uncertainty。And this course is unique so far as I know。

 because it brings the Bayesianological data analysis perspective down into the very first steps of data analysis before learners need to know any programming languages or advanced statistics。

 using simple， vivid examples requiring nothing more than algebra and offering realistic business problems that can be solved effectively。

 using only standard Microsoft Excel functions。I hope that this course helps to give you deep insights into the nature of information。

That will be of practical usefulness to you throughout your long and successful careers。
